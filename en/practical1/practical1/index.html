Step 12: Word embeddings (Word2Vec)¶

The previous steps treat words as symbols. Word embeddings (lecture 4, embeddings and Word2Vec) represent each word by a vector such that words used in similar contexts are close to each other.

  1. Split the page text into sentences and tokens (spaCy), then train a Word2Vec model with gensim (code below).

  2. Display the closest words of a few words from the page (most_similar) and comment: do the neighbours make sense? What happens with min_count=1 or epochs=5?

  3. Compare the Skip-gram (sg=1) and CBOW (sg=0) modes, as well as two window sizes (window=2 and window=10).

  4. Try a vector analogy (most_similar(positive=[...], negative=[...])) and explain why it works poorly on a single page.

  5. Optional: load fr_core_news_md (which ships vectors pre-trained on a large corpus) and compare, for the same words, the neighbours obtained with token.similarity to those of your model.

import spacy
from gensim.models import Word2Vec

nlp = spacy.load("fr_core_news_sm")
doc = nlp(text[:1_000_000])  # spaCy's default limit is 1,000,000 characters

# One list of lowercased alphabetic tokens per sentence
sentences = [[token.text.lower() for token in sent if token.is_alpha and not token.is_stop]
             for sent in doc.sents]
sentences = [s for s in sentences if len(s) > 2]
print(len(sentences), "sentences")

# Train Word2Vec (Skip-gram: sg=1; CBOW: sg=0) on the page
w2v = Word2Vec(sentences, vector_size=100, window=5, min_count=3, sg=1, epochs=50, seed=42)
print(len(w2v.wv), "words in the vocabulary")

for word in ["seine", "musée", "métro", "roi"]:
    if word in w2v.wv:
        print(word, "->", [w for w, _ in w2v.wv.most_similar(word, topn=5)])
